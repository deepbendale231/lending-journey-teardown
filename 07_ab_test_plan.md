# 07. A/B test plan

## Assumptions (stated, not measured)
The evidence is an outsider's walkthrough of the public journey, so these inputs are **assumptions** and are listed so they can be replaced:
- **Baseline completion rate 30%** (started to submitted). No funnel data was available. Replace it with the real figure; the sample size moves with it.
- **Minimum detectable lift +2 points.** The smallest change worth the cost of building the bet (about four person-months). A smaller lift would not repay it.
- **Baseline early delinquency 3%, tolerated worsening 0.5 points.** Illustrative; Risk owns the real margin.
- **Weekly eligible starts are unknown**, so duration is given as a formula, not a date.

The sample-size block below is generated by [tools/sample_size.py](tools/sample_size.py) from the front matter above; `make metrics` refreshes it and `make check` fails if it drifts.

## Hypothesis
Showing an **indicative eligibility range and rate band before the mobile-number gate** (story S8 in [05](05_user_stories.md)) will raise application completion without
raising early delinquency.

### Why this is the experiment, when RICE ranks it ninth
The cheap consistency fixes (stories S1 to S7) are **defects**: two pages contradict each other. They ship without a test, behind a flag and monitored. An experiment
earns its cost where the outcome is genuinely uncertain and the upside is large, and that is this change: it removes the requirement to give identity before seeing
any indication, but it also lets users who would not qualify opt out earlier, so completion could go either way, and credit quality could move. It ranks low on RICE
because it is expensive, not because it is unimportant; the test is how the bet gets de-risked before the money is spent at full scale.

## Design
| | |
|---|---|
| Variants | **Control:** current flow. **Treatment:** indicative range and total cost shown before the bureau consent step |
| Randomisation unit | **User** (stable anonymous id), assigned at first journey start and kept for the whole test, so a returning user never switches arm |
| Eligibility | New personal-loan journeys that reach the amount screen; excludes existing-customer pre-approved flows and internal test accounts |
| Allocation | 50 / 50 after a one-week 5% pilot that only checks instrumentation and guardrail plumbing |

## Metrics
| Role | Metric | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Completion rate | Users who submit an application ÷ users who start one, per user, within 7 days of first start |
| **Guardrail (early proxy)** | First-EMI default | Share of disbursed loans whose first EMI is missed; matures about 30-45 days after disbursal |
| **Guardrail (late)** | 30 DPD at 3 months | Read after the decision, never used to stop the test |
| Guardrail | Approval quality | Approval rate and mean bureau score of *submitted* applications: a rise in completion from weaker applicants is not a win |
| Guardrail | Complaint rate | Complaints per 1,000 started journeys |
| Guardrail | KFS-view rate | Share of sanctioned users who open the KFS before accepting: must not fall |

- Lehr's rule is the shortcut `n = 16 p(1-p) / delta²`; the exact normal approximation sits within a fraction of a percent of it.
- Randomise by user and run **whole weekly cycles** (at least two), so weekday and weekend behaviour are both covered.
- Every extra week of waiting for traffic is a week of cost: if `weekly_starts` makes the test too slow, **raise the minimum
  detectable effect** rather than cutting power.

## The delinquency guardrail takes months: how to read it
A delinquency outcome cannot stop a test that runs a few weeks. So:
1. **Gate on an early proxy** (first-EMI default, and early bureau-score movement) with a pre-set non-inferiority margin.
2. **Roll out in stages** (5% → 25% → 50% → 100%), each stage gated on the proxy.
3. **Keep a holdout** of treated users and read 30 and 60 DPD when they mature; the decision to stay at 100% is reviewed then.
4. Report the **confidence interval against the margin**, not a p-value: "the worst plausible worsening is X pts, inside our 0.5 pt margin".

## Stopping rules (written before the test)
- **Stop early for harm** if the upper bound of the one-sided 95% interval for (treatment − control) on first-EMI default exceeds the
  margin at any pre-registered look (weekly, at most four looks, α spent accordingly), or complaints per 1,000 are more than 50% above control.
- **Do not stop early for success.** Peeking at the primary metric and stopping when it looks good inflates false positives.
- **Sample-ratio mismatch check** every day: if the observed split differs from 50/50 by a chi-square p < 0.001, pause: the data is
  broken, not the product.

## Decision rule (pre-registered)
| Outcome | Condition (all must hold) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Ship** | Completion lift ≥ +1.0 pt with 95% CI above 0 · every guardrail inside its margin · no SRM | Ramp to 100%, keep the holdout, review late delinquency at 3 months |
| **Iterate** | Lift positive but CI includes 0 or lift < +1.0 pt · guardrails inside margin | Change the treatment (copy, range width), re-run; do not ship |
| **Kill** | Lift ≤ 0 · or any guardrail outside its margin · or SRM unresolved | Turn off, write up why, keep the instrumentation |

## Analysis plan
Two-proportion z-test and the confidence interval of the difference for completion. Pre-listed cuts only: new vs repeat device, salaried vs
self-employed, traffic source. Anything else is exploratory and labelled so.

## Instrumentation required
Events and properties are defined in [08_metrics_tree.md](08_metrics_tree.md). The test does not start until `journey_started`,
`journey_submitted`, `arm_assigned` and the guardrail events are verified end to end in the pilot week.

## Known limits
Novelty effects fade, so read the last full week separately. A completion lift that comes from weaker applicants shows up in approval
quality, not in the primary metric: that is why it is a guardrail. This plan cannot prove long-run credit performance.
